Research

Dynamic Trade, Spatial Macroeconomics & Geoeconomics

My research agenda lies at the intersection of international trade and macroeconomics. I study how economies adjust over time and across space to changes in market access, technology, and trade policy. My work brings growth and other macroeconomic dynamics into quantitative trade models, uses micro and spatial data to identify the underlying mechanisms, and extends this agenda to geoeconomics: how geopolitical power, conflict, and fragmentation shape trade and welfare.

01
Trade · Growth · InnovationWTO Essay Award 2024

Trade, Growth, and Product Innovation

How much of the gains from trade come from the creation of new products?

EU integration increased market access, product entry, R&D, and trade. I develop a dynamic general-equilibrium model combining Eaton–Kortum trade with Romer-style innovation to quantify static and dynamic gains from integration.

02
Dynamic Trade · Supply Chains

Dynamic Adjustment to Trade Shocks

with Junyuan Chen, Marc Muendler, and Fabian Trottner

Why does trade adjust slowly after shocks?

A model of staggered sourcing decisions rationalizes the gap between short- and long-run trade elasticities. Applied to the US–China trade war, adjustment frictions substantially amplify welfare losses.

Trade Policy · Geoeconomics

Trade Policy and Power Politics

with Philip Economides and Freddie Papazyan

How does geopolitical power change optimal trade policy?

We combine cross-country evidence on military power and tariffs with a quantitative trade model in which geopolitical alignment, coercive power, and conflict outside options shape tariff choices.

04
Trade · Growth · Geoeconomic Fragmentation

The Impact of Geopolitical Conflicts on Trade, Growth, and Innovation

with Eddy Bekkers and Ben Sutton

What are the long-run costs of fragmentation when trade also transmits ideas?

A multi-sector dynamic general-equilibrium model with international knowledge diffusion shows that decoupling can impose much larger welfare losses than conventional static trade models imply, particularly in lower-income regions.

PaperVoxEU
05
Trade · Labor Markets · Environment

Exports, Labor Markets, and the Environment: Evidence from Brazil

with Otavio Conceição, Gabriel Lara Ibarra, and Gladys Lopez-Acevedo

How do export shocks reshape local employment across environmentally risky and sustainable activities?

Using Brazilian customs, administrative, and census microdata, we estimate the dynamic local labor-market effects of export exposure and show that the environmental composition of employment changes substantially over the adjustment horizon.

Paper

Trade · Local Labor Markets · Spatial Adjustment

Local Labor Markets Dynamics and Export Shocks: Theory and Evidence from Indonesia

with Juan Segnana, Raymond Robertson, and Gladys Lopez-Acevedo

How do local labor markets adjust dynamically to foreign demand shocks?

We combine an instrument for export exposure based on foreign demand with a spatial framework and local labor-market evidence from Indonesia to trace the dynamic responses of employment, income, and welfare.

Paper
07
Trade Rerouting · Tariffs · Supply ChainsIn progress

Trade Rerouting: Theory and Evidence

with Jose Moran

When tariffs rise, how much trade is rerouted through third countries?

We model indirect sourcing through an entrepôt and study how rerouting attenuates tariff pass-through and welfare losses, then test the mechanism using firm-level Mexican trade data around the 2018 U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods.

08
Spatial Economics · Inequality · Top IncomesIn progress

The Economic Geography of Top Incomes

Why are top earners so geographically concentrated?

Using U.S. county-level administrative tax data, we measure representation in the national top 1 percent and relate its spatial concentration to local industrial composition and exposure to inequality-intensive industries.

09
Fiscal Policy · Inequality · Spatial MacroeconomicsIn progress

Tax Multipliers in the United States: A Regional Perspective

with Edoardo Briganti and Victor Sellemi

Does the local effect of a federal tax reform depend on who bears the tax change?

We combine county income distributions with federal tax reforms to construct heterogeneous local tax shocks and estimate their effects on employment, consumption, and local economic activity.
