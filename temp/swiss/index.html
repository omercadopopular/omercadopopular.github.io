Carlos Góes

Dynamic Trade, Spatial Macroeconomics & Geoeconomics

My research lies at the intersection of international trade and macroeconomics. I bring the dynamics of growth and adjustment emphasized in macroeconomics into quantitative trade models, and the spatial structure emphasized in trade into macroeconomic analysis. My empirical work uses micro and spatial data to identify adjustment across firms, workers, and places. A growing part of this agenda studies how geopolitical power, conflict, and fragmentation reshape trade policy, market access, innovation, and welfare.

Trade · Growth · Innovation

Trade, Growth, and Product Innovation

How much of the gains from trade come from the creation of new products?

EU integration increased market access, product entry, R&D, and trade. I develop a dynamic general-equilibrium model combining Eaton–Kortum trade with Romer-style innovation to quantify static and dynamic gains from integration.

02
Trade · Growth · Fragmentation

The Impact of Geopolitical Conflicts on Trade, Growth, and Innovation

with Eddy Bekkers and Ben Sutton

How large are the long-run costs of persistent geopolitical fragmentation?

We develop a multi-sector, multi-region model with dynamic knowledge diffusion to study geopolitical decoupling. Fragmentation reduces trade and the international diffusion of ideas, producing substantially larger and more uneven welfare losses than in conventional static trade models.

03
Dynamic Trade · Supply Chains

Dynamic Adjustment to Trade Shocks

with Junyuan Chen, Marc Muendler, and Fabian Trottner

Why does trade adjust slowly after shocks?

A model of staggered sourcing decisions rationalizes the gap between short- and long-run trade elasticities. Applied to the US–China trade war, adjustment frictions substantially amplify welfare losses and alter the short- and long-run incidence on third countries.

04
Trade Policy · Geoeconomics

Trade Policy and Power Politics

with Philip Economides and Freddie Papazyan

How does geopolitical power change optimal trade policy?

We combine cross-country evidence on military power and tariffs with a quantitative trade model in which governments choose trade policy under the shadow of conflict and geopolitical alignment. Power changes disagreement payoffs, tariff incentives, and the feasibility of peaceful cooperation.

05
Trade · Labor Markets · Environment

Exports, Labor Markets, and the Environment: Evidence from Brazil

with O. Conceição, G. Lara Ibarra, and G. Lopez-Acevedo

How do local labor markets adjust to export growth, and how does that adjustment differ across environmentally risky and sustainable activities?

Using Brazilian customs, administrative, and census microdata, we estimate persistent local employment responses to export shocks and document different short- and long-run adjustment patterns across environmentally risky and sustainable activities.

02 / Policy

Policy, institutions & public debate

I have worked on economic policy from inside government and multilateral institutions, advised decision-makers, and written for broader public audiences alongside my academic research.

My policy work has centered on translating quantitative economics into decisions: diagnosing policy problems, building empirical and structural evidence, and communicating the resulting trade-offs to policymakers and the public.

This has included work on trade reform in the Brazilian government, research and policy analysis at the IMF, WTO, World Bank and IFC, and op-eds, interviews, and public-facing economic writing.
